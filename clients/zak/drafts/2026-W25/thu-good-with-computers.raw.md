# RAW — Thursday Personal Story: "You seem good with computers" — 2026-W25

> Working raw file. Original draft + teardown diagnosis. Deepen via Socratic questioning BEFORE rewriting.

## Original draft (verbatim)
Someone told me you seem good with computers, I was intrigued. I'd been speaking to someone about what we do at SAWA (helping creator-preneurs and startups turn their email lists into revenue) and they ended up stating that I seem good with computers. It triggered many thoughts, but one that stuck: you can be good at something and still not succeed. Facets: (1) we get complacent/comfortable when we get compliments; compliments indicate you're likely heading the right way, but recognize it, reconcile you still have more to do, don't get presumptuous. (2) nobody keeps score of our effort — harsh but liberating; no one sees the 12-hour days, the sleepless nights; the only proof it happened is to eventually succeed. "Notes to self... but I'd love to hear your thoughts."

## Teardown diagnosis
- **THE BIG FLOP:** the hook writes a check the post doesn't cash. "You seem good with computers" is a specific, intriguing, slightly *reductive* compliment — it sets up a story about being misperceived/underestimated (you described a real business, they flattened it to "good with computers"). Then the body abandons that and pivots to two generic self-help lessons. The most interesting, true, specific thing is the comment itself — and he walked past it.
- **Two posts in one:** "don't get complacent from compliments" and "nobody scores your effort" are separate ideas; neither gets developed. One idea per post.
- **Too abstract:** all aphorism, no scene/number/stakes. Violates his own substance rule (tactical/specific > broad/inspirational).
- **Hidden contradiction worth mining:** "nobody keeps score of effort, only success counts" is outcome-worship — the opposite of his recent posts on detaching from outcomes / process & experimentation mattering. That tension could BE the substance.
- **Hedged close:** "notes to self… I'd love to hear your thoughts" undercuts conviction.

## Deepening (Socratic Q&A)

### Round 1 (verbatim)
- **Decision:** DROP the second piece ("nobody sees the effort"). Single idea.
- **The moment:** it didn't sting. It felt like a compliment, but also like being **reduced to just "good with computers"** — whilst business requires being good at many things and wearing many hats.
- **The real twist (the gold):** it triggered the fact that he's **actually not good at computers.** "I'm not technical, but rather someone who is learning them. In this day and age of AI, because tech has been more or less democratized, I do what I can with what I have."
- He didn't expect the person to have full context on Sawa; even if it feels like a misunderstanding, he didn't take it personally. Not a conundrum.
- **Outcome discernment (his resolution to the contradiction):** both matter — (1) outcome counts, we have to work for outcomes; (2) we also have to be outcome-agnostic, not married to the outcome. A balanced approach, doing both to maximize the upside.

### Sharpened angle (THIS post)
A stranger called me "good with computers." Two things hit me: (1) it quietly reduced what running a business actually takes — you wear ten hats, none of them is "computers"; (2) the funny part — I'm not even good with computers. I'm not technical. I'm someone learning, doing what I can with what I have, in an age where AI handed all of us tools we didn't have before. Ties straight to his story (not-technical → building anyway → "the way appears").

### Recommendation
- Make THIS post the "good with computers" irony (reduced + not-actually-technical + AI democratization). One idea.
- The **outcome-discernment** (work for outcomes AND stay outcome-agnostic) is a *separate, strong post* — it resolves the tension across his recent posts. Don't bolt it on here (that re-creates the two-ideas problem). Save it.

### Still open
- The concrete "I do what I can with what I have" proof (likely: can't code traditionally, but vibe-codes / ships agents with AI).
- The "many hats" Sawa actually requires.
