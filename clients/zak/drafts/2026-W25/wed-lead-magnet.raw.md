## Deepening (Socratic Q&A — verbatim)

### Round 1
**Q1 — define the two types:**
- **Evergreen** = a thorough, deep one. It's usually a *step of the entire delivery process*. If your delivery is A→Z, the evergreen lead magnet is step A, B, or C. You give it to prospects to help them solve one problem, which creates the need for the subsequent solutions.
- **Viral/engagement** = doesn't have to be thorough or deep. Could be a swipe file, something you curate, or something not even inside your delivery system. (e.g. you talk tech/AI but have a lead magnet for career advice.) You churn more of these because they're low-lift, or you make them low-lift. Audit, sample, whatever — it has to be low-lift.

**Q2 — examples:**
- Evergreen: a **7-day crash course** we worked on; got really positive feedback on the material.
- Viral: we came up with ideas by looking at the creator's **ManyChat** and the conversion rate on his reel-to-DM ("real to DM") posts. We went after the higher click-rate videos and turned those into viral lead magnets.

**Q3 — small list, which first:**
- Start with an **evergreen** one. Put a lot of work into it, but keep a **regular cadence** supplementing it with viral ones.
- "On steroids": on your viral lead magnet, connect people to / have them check your evergreen one. Now they give in, you hit them with extra value, and there's a clear funnel to check your services/products.

**Q4 — underrated by whom / the mistake:**
- More underrated among **companies** than creators. Not all creators leverage their content + expertise and package it as a lead magnet.
- The mistake: thinking of it as a **one-off**. It's a **cyclical process** — your content informs your lead magnet, your lead magnet informs your content, which informs your products/services/future features.

**Q5 — the chain (and mistake #2):**
- Someone checks a lead magnet. It has to be really good (get feedback, prompt them for it). They might not buy instantly.
- Wrong way people think: "if I have a lead magnet, people should buy from me." Not the case. Raising their hand for the magnet is a **first signal**, not proof they're in-market now. Some are (~**1%**), but not always.
- So always: **keep nurturing, keep feeding them more lead magnets, ask them questions, add them to your email list** — and when it makes sense, they buy. You're increasing **mindshare** so that when they're on the market, you're the go-to guy.

**Q6 — the lever:**
- Yes, **naming the pain + triggering curiosity** has to be really good. [likely "It can't be fluff, can't be AI slop, can't be something you looked up online"] — it has to be genuinely **valuable and demonstrate your expertise**.
- It could save them time (a swipe file curated over years), something that feels like second nature to you that few have. It has to be good. You only know via feedback — asking if it's good / if there's interest, or people responding "this is really good."

**Q7 — relation to paid product:**
- You can take **one lead magnet and test different packaging** — name it 3 different ways and see which works.
- **Not all lead magnets should be aligned to the paid product.** You don't want to always make the ask. Sometimes you just want to give.
