## Deepening (Socratic Q&A — verbatim)

### Round 1
1. **Trigger:** "We launched a coaching offer for one of our clients and it didn't perform as we wanted, so it triggered that and made me think about outcomes and business outcomes."
2. **The reframe (core):** Yes, I'm a breadwinner building a business and outcomes should matter enormously to me. Deeper layer: am I trying to detach from the outcome itself, or from what NOT getting the result makes me believe about myself? Truth: the outcome still matters. The money still matters, the paychecks still matter. But for the short term, recognizing that business — especially in the early days, months, even years — might not be outcome-generating is something we have to reconcile. This is NOT sunk-cost fallacy (investing in a thing despite it not working). It's: do enormous input and still not expect something very high, because we're experimenting. Being so attached to outcomes (short run) makes me think very short term, kills longevity thinking, and doesn't even push me to stop a venture/business when I should — which is not ideal.
4. **What detachment looks like (behavioral):** "You do the input, you expect something, but when that thing doesn't happen you don't become sad and disappointed — you become grateful, you pick up the learnings, and you move on."
5. **The learning piece:** "Stepping back, taking a massive step back, recognizing how far I've come and cherishing that, but conversely taking a different approach to achieving that outcome."

### Sharpened thesis (to confirm)
Detachment isn't *not caring* about outcomes (I'm a breadwinner; they matter). It's detaching my **self-worth** from the result — especially in the experimental early stage. Pour in massive input, hold the outcome loosely, and when it doesn't land, be grateful, take the learnings, and either step back and rebuild or have the clarity to stop. Attachment distorts judgment in both directions: short-term thinking, and clinging when you should walk away.

### Center of the post (the tension to hold)
Detach your **self-worth** from the outcome so a flop doesn't wreck you — but don't let that detachment become an excuse to **bail too early**. "Success and failure have the same path; failure is just an earlier exit." The skill is discerning when "this isn't working" is real vs. just disappointment talking.
